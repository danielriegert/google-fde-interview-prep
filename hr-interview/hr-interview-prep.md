# Questions

1. What does a typical engagement look like — how long, and do FDEs work solo on an account or as part of a pod with a PM or sales engineer? Type of problems being addressed e.g.g focus purely on GenAI or also adjacent areas e.g. data pipelines, etc.

2. The role talks about converting field patterns into product feature requests — has that actually landed? Is there a formal channel into the Vertex or Gemini teams, and can you point to something the field asked for that shipped?

3. "Can you walk me through the loop — how many rounds, and what's in each? I've read the FDE format is newer, so I'd rather hear it from you than from the internet."
   Highest-value question you can ask her, and it's exactly what she offered to help with. Follow-ups worth getting: is there a DS&A screen or is it all applied? Language of choice? Any customer-facing or discovery-simulation round? Listen for: whether there's a whiteboard algorithms round — that changes your prep entirely.

4. "For candidates who've gone through this loop and not made it, where does it usually break down?"
   Recruiters almost always answer this honestly and specifically, and almost nobody asks it. You'll get the real bar. Listen for: if she says "coding depth," you know where to spend the next three weeks; if she says "couldn't handle ambiguity" or "too advisory, not enough building," that's a different prep entirely — and a hint about how your profile will be read.

5. "On compensation — could you break out how the package is structured? Base, bonus target, and how the initial equity grant compares to the refresh cycle."
   She's already said she'll cover comp, so this just makes sure you get the structure rather than a headline number. Listen for: the initial GSU grant, which is where the negotiable room usually sits. Don't name your own number in this call if you can avoid it
